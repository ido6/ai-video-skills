## 1. Three principles everything derives from

1. **Every move has a trigger.** A move is motivated when something in the scene sets it off: a character moves, a look, a sound, a turn. Unmotivated motion makes the viewer notice the camera. **[book: Brown, Katz]**
2. **Motion only reads against stillness.** Contrast raises intensity, sameness lowers it. One locked shot inside a handheld film is the loudest shot. **[book: Block]**
3. **A move changes one of three things:** what's in frame (pan, tilt), how big it is (push, pull, zoom), or the relation between depth planes (track, arc, crane). Only the third creates parallax, a depth cue. **[book: Block]**

## 2. Movement glossary

| Move | What it says | When motivated | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pan** | Following, surveying, linking two things in one space | Character moves or looks. Second point of interest | Pan with no target to land on. In 9:16 every pan feels faster |
| **Tilt** | Height, scale, reveal head-to-toe or product-to-face | Vertical subject, look up/down | Tilting architecture so verticals converge |
| **Whip pan** | Energy, urgency, cause→effect between two subjects. Also hides a cut | Head turn, sound or reaction pulls attention | No framed subject at start/end. Shallow focus landing soft |
| **Dolly / track** | Travel with someone. Depth and presence | Character moves. Need to reveal geography | Subject size "pulses" during the move |
| **Push-in** | Entering the character's space: realisation, decision, rising importance | Emotional turn, reveal | Confusing with zoom. Pushing on every beat until meaningless |
| **Pull-out** | Isolation, context, release, ending | New info around the character | Revealing an undesigned space |
| **Crane / jib** | Scale, arrival, wide-to-scene transition | Entering/leaving the world | Height change that doesn't change what we see |
| **Steadicam** | Graceful continuity, dreamy follow, long takes | Long travel with a character | Floating horizon. "Hovering" feel when tension was wanted |
| **Gimbal** | Fluid energy, closeness to moving subject | Tight spaces, fast moves | Robotic perfection, no weight |
| **Handheld (shoulder)** | Immediacy, subjectivity, tension. In quiet scenes: intimacy | Scene needs presence or unease | Shake by default until it says nothing |
| **Zoom** | Emphasis, observation, "looking at" | Point at something without entering its space | Calling a push-in a zoom or vice versa |
| **Snap zoom** | Comic punch, reaction emphasis, doc feel | Punchline, sudden realisation | Using it in restrained luxury without meaning a joke |
| **Dolly zoom** | Shock, vertigo | One decisive moment at most | Decoration |
| **Rack focus** | Moves attention A→B within one frame | Look, sound, entrance | Too much DoF. No composed target |
| **Arc / orbit** | Examination, glamour, turning point | Character still, her moment | Long arc revealing undesigned space. Accidental line cross |
| **Follow / lead** | Follow: go where she goes, face hidden. Lead: read the face as the world passes | Character walks somewhere that matters | Drifting distance so shot size changes |

**Zoom vs push-in:** push-in approaches and has parallax; zoom observes and has none. **Never mix them in one shot.** **[pro + secondary]**

## 3. Handheld that reads cinematic and tense, not amateur

| Principle | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Mass** | Heavier camera is steadier and "anchors" the shot | Roger Deakins |
| **Walking** | Bent knees as shock absorbers. Walking backwards on a flat surface is normal for a skilled operator | Deakins |
| **Isolation** | Good operator isolates camera from body motion, **including breathing**. So visible "breathing" is a stylistic choice, not a realism marker | ASC |
| **Contact points** | On shoulder, handles close to body: slow weighted float. Held in hands: fast jitter | secondary |
| **Focal length** | Wide-to-normal hides shake. Long lenses magnify it | secondary |
| **Shutter** | 180° keeps natural blur. Narrow shutter + handheld = staccato combat look | ASC, RED |
| **Reason** | Frame moves because something happened, then settles. Indiscriminate shake is the known mistake | secondary |
| **Don't stabilise lateral motion in post** | Foreground and background stop moving relative to each other; looks unnatural | Deakins |

**How to ask for it:** "shoulder-mounted weight, slow float, reactive reframes, settles when she stops", with a normal or slightly wide lens. **[rec]**

**For tension:** tension doesn't come from constant shake. It comes from late-reacting framing, short-siding, closeness, and contrast with a still shot.

## 4. Composition

| Principle | What it does |
|---|---|
| Rule of thirds | Default off-centre placement. Gives looking room |
| Hitchcock rule | Size in frame = importance at that moment |
| Headroom | Small consistent space above head. Shrinks as shot tightens |
| Lead / looking room | Space in direction of gaze or motion. More when moving |
| Short-siding | Character pressed to the edge they face. Reads trapped/isolated. **Tension tool** |
| Centre framing | Control and dominance |
| Balance | Symmetric: calm, ordered. Asymmetric: dynamic. Unbalanced: tension |
| Depth | Fore/mid/background. Deep space more intense, flat space calm and graphic |
| Leading lines | Pull eye to subject. Diagonals more intense than horizontals/verticals |
| Frame within frame | Doors, arches, mirrors isolate subject and add depth layer |
| Negative space | Emptiness around subject. More = more unease |
| Dirty single | Soft foreground shoulder ties two people into one space |
| Eyeline | Closer to lens = more intimate. Eyelines must match across cuts |

**Symmetry & luxury architecture:** symmetric, level, centred frame = "formal balance". Suits classical spaces and makes the handheld shots after it feel less stable. **[rec, per Mascelli & Block]**

### What changes in 9:16
- Frame is narrow, easily filled by one subject. Strong verticals (standing figure, doors, columns, bottle) justify the format.
- **Horizontal motion is exaggerated in a narrow frame.** Fast pan disorients; tilt suits better. Handheld shake is amplified too.
- Low and high angles gain power.
- **Safe zones (Meta, per secondary sources):** no text, logo or key element in the top 14%, bottom 35%, or 6% each side. **[not verified against Meta's page]** At 1080×1920 that leaves a safe box of ~950×979 px, centred ~40% from top, not geometric centre. **[calc]**

**Placement rules [rec]:**
- Eyes on the upper third line.
- Hero product centred ~40% from top.
- Nothing essential in the bottom third. It's still image — use it for foreground texture: floor reflections, soft tray edge.
- **Two characters don't fit side by side.** Stack them in depth (one near & low, one far & high) and shift attention with rack focus or a short pan.
- Depth comes from the Z axis: stage walks toward/away from lens, not across.
- Almost no horizontal lead room. Centre for control, slight offset for tension.

## 5. Lenses & optics

- **Perspective comes from camera position, not focal length.** "Compression" is the result of standing farther away. **[secondary + Brown]**
- **Perceived speed:** wide lens up close exaggerates motion to/from camera. Long lens flattens it and exaggerates lateral motion and pan speed.
- **Depth of field** depends on T-stop, focal length, focus distance and format. Large format looks shallower because the same FOV needs a longer lens.
- **Spherical vs anamorphic:** for 9:16, spherical. Anamorphic exists to widen the image. **[rec]**
- **Rack focus** needs shallow DoF, two clearly separated planes, and a trigger.
- **Shutter:** 180° at 24 fps is standard. Narrower: sharp staccato motion, every particle visible (also broken glass). **Whip pan needs the opposite:** 180° so the pan smears.
- **Flicker:** in 50 Hz countries (e.g. Paris) use 1/50 or 1/100 s. At 24 fps that's a 172.8° shutter. **[calc]**
- **Slow motion:** one shot per film (the hook), everything else real-time, so the contrast works. **[rec]** A glass falling 1.2 m is airborne 0.49 s (12 frames) and lands at 4.9 m/s. A 0.25 s impact-and-scatter lasts 2.1 s on screen at 200 fps. For a 2–3 s hook shot the useful range is 200–300 fps. **[calc]**

### Previs bridge: millimetres only mean something with a gate
Blender with Sensor Fit "Auto" applies sensor size to the larger dimension. In 9:16 with the default 36 mm sensor the image is 36 mm tall × 20.25 mm wide: **a full-frame camera on its side.**

ARRI ALEXA Mini LF rotated 90° uses 36.70×20.64 mm for 9:16. So Blender focal lengths are real focal lengths of that package within 2%. For exact match set sensor to 36.7.

## 7. Directing with the camera

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| **180° line** | Axis between two subjects or along motion. Stay on one side and screen direction holds |
| **Neutral shots** | Frontal and from-behind shots carry no left/right and can bridge a change |
| **Crossing the line** | Must be seen: camera travels across it, or a neutral shot bridges |
| **30° and 20% rule** | Between two shots of the same subject change angle ≥30° and size noticeably, else it reads as a jump cut |
| **Match on action** | Cut during motion and continue it in the next shot. Motion hides the cut |
| **Eyeline match** | Look out, then what's looked at. Directions must match geography |
| **Eye-trace** | If the viewer's eye is in the same place before/after the cut it's smooth; if it must jump, it's harsh. In 9:16 keep the point of interest at the same height across cuts unless you want a jolt |
| **Murch's rule of six** | Cut priorities: emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye-trace 7%, 2D plane 5%, 3D space 4%. Sacrifice from the bottom |
| **Shifting attention in one take** | Pan or whip pan, rack focus, character entering/exiting, size change |
| **Reaction shot** | Completes cause→effect and carries emotion |

**Oner vs coverage:** a oner keeps real time and geography and builds tension, but everything must work at once and editing can't fix pace. **In AI [rec]:** a generated oner must hold identity, geography and physics in every beat, so one failed beat kills the whole take. Cuts isolate risk per shot. Use a oner only when continuous time is the point.

## 8. Ad & fragrance conventions

- **Hook:** TikTok: prefer the hook in the first 6 s and show the offer in 3. Meta: Reels play with sound on by default. YouTube: get to story fast, tight framing, show product early. **[maker]**
- **What holds attention:** clear sense of place, characters with agency, an unfolding scene, melodic music — beat flat, abstract, text-led work. **[pro: Orlando Wood]** Supports a small continuous scene over a montage.
- **Luxury restraint:** in luxury strategy, advertising feeds the dream, not the sale. In camera language: few shots, long enough to read, shallow focus, practical light, no sales graphics on the image. **[rec, per Kapferer]**
- **Packshot:** shows product and label clearly. Glass and liquid depend on backlight. A reflective object is lit via what it reflects. A glossy surface underneath gives a reflection.
- **Packshot build [rec]:** long or macro lens. Mechanical camera, not handheld. Slow eased move, or none. Product upright in the safe box. **Final frame held ~2 s.** Background from the film's own world.
- **Pace [rec]:** 4–6 shots in 15–20 s ≈ 3–4 s per shot average. No reliable published norm found.

## 9. Lighting that drives camera decisions

- **Motivated light:** light seems to come from a source the audience believes: lamp, window, chandelier.
- **Practicals set the tone, then get help:** a little bounce extends what the practical does. For a ballroom: light LED rings on existing chandeliers. **[Deakins]**
- **Colour temperature:** candle ~1,800K. Household incandescent 2,700–2,800K. Dimmed ~2,200K. Studio tungsten 3,200K.
- **Warm night interior:** warmth comes from sources and camera white balance together. **Describe as tungsten practicals at ~2,700K seen through a camera balanced to 3,200–3,600K.** **[rec, per Deakins & Mullen]**
- **Contrast ratio:** key:fill 2:1 low, 4:1 medium, 8:1 dramatic. Typical: 4:1 on the face, deeper in the background. **[rec]**
- **Consistency:** light direction is part of continuity. If key comes from chandeliers camera-left in one shot, same in the next.
- **Camera consequences:** practical-only light means T2–T2.8 and shallow focus. Sources in frame need highlight range and bloom under diffusion. High speed needs its own flicker-free light.

## 10. How this translates to previs & prompt

1. **Numbers come only from the previs.** Never write a focal length that doesn't exist on the Blender camera.
2. **One package per film**, written once in the prompt's look block, identical in every shot. It's a look anchor; geometry comes from the blockout.
3. **One move per shot.** A complex move gets split by a cut hidden inside a whip.
4. **Lens or height changes** between shots are declared in the shot plan so they don't read as drift.
5. **Generate in establishing order, edit in hook order.**
6. **Slow motion** declared explicitly, only for the shot that needs it. Everything else real-time.
7. **Niche term** written with its visible result.
